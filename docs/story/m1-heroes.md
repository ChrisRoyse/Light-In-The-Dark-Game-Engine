# M1 Heroes -- Four Psychological Profiles

Section of the Milestone-1 story bible (docs/story/m1-story-bible.md). Doctrine: #1848.
Hero names, primary attributes, and ability names are phase-1 locked (faction_data.rs).
This file gives psychology; it does not change locked data.

Method note. Each profile is built to the research (see docs/story/m1-influence-log.md):
a want (external, conscious, drives plot) versus a need (internal, often unconscious,
the real growth), a specific "Lie the character believes" born of a specific wound, and
a deliberate ATTACHMENT HOOK made of three observable ingredients -- competence,
vulnerability, and a loyalty display -- because those are what the parasocial-attachment
and permadeath-grief studies show actually make an audience bond and then grieve. Each
hero also has an explicit stance toward the Unturning (irreversibility), and the four
stances are deliberately different: one accepts it, one rages against it, one reveres
it, one is undone by it.

Attachment-hook engineering, in short: players bond to a character who is visibly GOOD
at something (competence), who can visibly be HURT or is privately afraid (vulnerability),
and who visibly puts someone else first at a cost (loyalty). Grief at a permanent loss
scales with the strength of that bond; so we build the bond hardest on the character we
will take away (Miren) and on the two the player must keep past the loss (Doran, Yvenne).

## Emberhold Compact

### Doran Ashheart -- the one who ACCEPTS irreversibility
Primary: STR. Abilities (locked): Emberbrand Strike, Bulwark Stance, Molten Wrath.
Role: the wall that leads. First-mission protagonist.

- Want (external): hold Ashford Crossing and wall it in stone, so Corramere's heart is
  never lost again.
- Need (internal): to let something in -- to accept help, to grieve out loud, to open a
  gate in the wall he has made of himself.
- The Lie he believes: "If I build it strong enough, no one I love dies." Born of the
  wound that as a boy he survived the Guttering behind the one stretch of wall that held,
  while those in the open did not; he decided the wall saved him and openness killed them,
  and has been building walls ever since.
- Core flaw: he fortifies instead of feeling. He walls his heart exactly as he walls a
  town; he cannot mourn, only reinforce. His acceptance of the Unturning is real but
  incomplete -- he accepts that the past cannot change, and uses that as a reason never to
  look at it.
- Stance toward the Unturning: acceptance, even comfort. "What is done is done, so build
  the next thing higher." He is the most at peace with irreversibility and the least at
  peace with grief -- the campaign teaches him these are not the same thing.
- Attachment hook:
  - Competence: he is unshakeable on defense; his Bulwark Stance visibly saves the line;
    troops rally where he stands.
  - Vulnerability: alone, he cannot say the names of those he lost in the Guttering; he
    starts and stops. The unbreakable man cannot speak one sentence.
  - Loyalty display: he stands the last watch himself so his people can sleep; he takes
    the exposed post at Ashford so Miren does not have to.
- Arc: Miren's death (Mission 3) is the wall he could not build high enough. He does not
  get to un-happen it (Unturning); he has to do the thing he never could -- grieve, and
  then open the gate to the enemy who share his loss. He carries the loss into the peace
  he builds; the wall he raises at the end has a gate.

### Sister Miren -- the one who is UNDONE by irreversibility (the permanent loss)
Primary: INT. Abilities (locked): Warding Light, Cinder Mend, Radiant Bastion.
Role: the healer and keeper of the line. She dies, permanently, in Mission 3.

- Want (external): save every soul she is responsible for -- lose no one, ever.
- Need (tragic): to accept that she cannot save everyone -- a need she never fully
  reaches, which is what makes her a tragedy rather than a lesson. Her greatness and her
  doom are the same trait.
- The Lie she believes: "If I am good enough and give enough, no loss is final." She
  treats the Unturning as an enemy to be out-mended. Wound: as a young chaplain she
  lowered a ward one moment too early to save her strength, and someone she could have
  sheltered died; she swore she would never again spend too little.
- Core flaw: she cannot leave a loss un-mended, and will overspend herself past the
  point of return to prevent one. The very oath-logic of wardsmithing (a ward sworn over
  the living cannot be lowered while any it shelters still stands) turns her virtue into
  the instrument of her death.
- Stance toward the Unturning: refusal, quietly and heroically. She spends her whole
  life arguing with a law that does not negotiate.
- Attachment hook (built hardest, because she is the loss):
  - Competence: she is the reason the army survives attrition; her Cinder Mend and
    Radiant Bastion are the difference between a costly win and a wipe. The player leans
    on her every mission she is present.
  - Vulnerability: she is physically fragile (low HP, locked), and she carries the guilt
    of the ward she lowered too early; in a quiet scene she confesses she is more afraid
    of failing to save someone than of dying.
  - Loyalty display: she is the one who goes back for the stragglers, always; her whole
    behavior is putting others before herself, visibly, every mission.
- Her death (the irreversible event): at Ashford she raises a Radiant Bastion over a
  column of refugees crossing under fire. By the oath-logic, she cannot lower it while
  they still stand within it. The player is given every tool and cannot save her -- the
  situation is engineered so that the only way the refugees live is if she holds the ward
  to the last, and she chooses to. She dies holding it. She cannot be revived: there is
  nothing left to bring forward; she spent all of herself and, by the Unturning, spent
  cannot be un-spent. She stays dead through the finale. This is the campaign's emotional
  spine and doctrine rule 3 made flesh.

## Veilspun Court

### Yvenne Duskblade -- the one who RAGES against irreversibility
Primary: AGI. Abilities (locked): Shadowstep, Venom Kiss, Umbral Flurry.
Role: the Court's fastest blade; the perspective the campaign shifts to mid-arc.

- Want (external): strike the Emberhold force she blames for pressing the Court toward
  ruin, and lose no one of hers ever again -- to be fast enough that death never catches
  what she guards.
- Need (internal): to stop running and stand still inside a grief she cannot outrun; to
  accept a death she could not prevent instead of avenging it forever.
- The Lie she believes: "If I am fast enough, I can prevent the loss." Wound: she was a
  child-guardian of the hidden remnant and once, being an instant too slow, lost one of
  her charges; she has mistaken motion for control ever since.
- Core flaw: she confuses speed with safety and vengeance with justice; she outruns
  reflection. In a world ruled by the Unturning, believing you can outrun consequence is
  the deepest possible error -- nothing in Oruvenn can be outrun.
- Stance toward the Unturning: rage and denial. She hates the law and lives as if it can
  be beaten by being quick enough. The campaign is the education of that rage.
- Attachment hook:
  - Competence: dazzling mobility; Shadowstep and Umbral Flurry let the player pull off
    saves and assassinations that feel like skill rewarded.
  - Vulnerability: she is fragile (glass), and under the speed she is terrified of being
    too slow again; she does not sleep.
  - Loyalty display: she throws herself between danger and the remnant's children without
    hesitation; her recklessness is always in someone else's defense.
- Arc and the tragic hinge: it is Yvenne's ambush, launched in fog against what she
  believes is an Emberhold war-march, that turns out to be the refugee column Miren dies
  shielding (Mission 4 reveals this from her side -- the recognition/reversal). She, who
  rages against irreversible loss, has now caused one she can never take back. Her arc is
  from vengeance to the harder thing: helping build a peace that honors a death she is
  partly responsible for, without any hope of undoing it.

### Caul the Whisperer -- the one who REVERES irreversibility
Primary: INT. Abilities (locked): Gloom Bolt, Veil of Night, Whispered Doom.
Role: keeper of the Court's memory and its secrets; the mind that brokers the end.

- Want (external): keep the hidden remnant unfound and the Court's secrets kept, by any
  concealment, whatever the cost to the living.
- Need (internal): to let one true thing out of the dark and into the light -- to trust,
  once, that a revealed truth can save rather than doom.
- The Lie he believes: "A secret kept is a life saved; the light is where death finds
  you." Wound: he is the Whisperer who keeps the memory-cloth; he has spent his life
  making sure no dead soul is ever un-remembered, and has come to serve the dead's
  secrets over the living's needs.
- Core flaw: he manipulates and withholds "for their protection," and lets the living
  suffer to keep the dead's shadows. He reveres the Unturning so deeply -- every name
  written forever, nothing lost -- that he has made a prison of remembrance.
- Stance toward the Unturning: reverence. To Caul the fact that nothing can be un-written
  is holy; it is why he keeps every name. His growth is not to stop revering it but to
  see that honoring the unchangeable dead cannot mean lying to the changeable living.
- Attachment hook:
  - Competence: he sees three moves ahead; Veil of Night and Whispered Doom turn a losing
    field; the player wins by his cunning.
  - Vulnerability: he is old and tired and carries every name he has ever kept; he fears
    that if he ever stops remembering, the dead die twice.
  - Loyalty display: he has kept the remnant's location secret through torture-worthy
    pressure; his loyalty is total and self-erasing.
- Arc: at the finale it is Caul who must do the thing his whole life forbade -- reveal a
  kept truth (that both peoples guard the same buried heart, that the Court's remnant and
  the Emberhold's dead draw warmth from the same source) into the open, to Doran, to make
  the shared peace possible. He lets one truth into the light and it saves rather than
  dooms -- and Miren still stays dead, because reverence for the Unturning means the peace
  is built on the loss, never over it.

## The four stances, side by side

- Doran: accepts the past cannot change -- and hides from grief inside that acceptance.
- Miren: refuses that any loss is final -- and is destroyed by the law she argues with.
- Yvenne: rages that loss cannot be undone -- and causes the very loss she cannot undo.
- Caul: reveres that nothing is un-written -- and imprisons the living to serve the dead.

Each learns the same lesson from the opposite direction: in a world of the Unturning, you
cannot take anything back, so the only freedom is what you choose to build, reveal, stand,
or forgive NEXT -- carrying the loss forward, never erasing it.
