# M1 World Frame -- Oruvenn and the Unturning

Section of the Milestone-1 story bible (docs/story/m1-story-bible.md). Doctrine: #1848.
ASCII-only. Original expression; see docs/story/m1-influence-log.md for pattern sources.

## 1. The world: Oruvenn

Oruvenn is the known reach of a single temperate country -- a land of cold rivers,
ash-grey moors, banked forge-towns, and hill-shadow. It is not a cosmology of gods
warring in a pantheon; it is a small, human-scaled world where two peoples share one
grief. The scale of M1 is deliberately intimate: one people, one wound, one crossing.
Larger cosmology is left unwritten so later milestones can extend without contradiction.

Technology is medieval: stone, timber, iron, torsion engines, water and forge heat.
Gunpowder is the hard ceiling (#1848 rule 2) and is not reached: no firearms, no
cannon, no industry, no electricity. The Emberhold Siege Ballista is a torsion machine;
the Veilspun Gloomwing Raider is a glider-borne skirmisher, not an aircraft. Any
advancement beyond the medieval baseline is a case-by-case operator review; none is
proposed in this campaign.

## 2. The first law: the Unturning

The organizing metaphysical fact of Oruvenn -- of its physics, its magic, its religion,
and its mechanics -- is that nothing turns back. This is called the Unturning. Time is
strictly forward. No spell, no rite, no relic, no hero, and no prayer can reverse,
rewind, alter, or un-happen any event. What is done is done. A deed, once done, stands
written for good.

This is the world model agreeing with the engine. The simulation is a deterministic,
append-only order-stream: history is a ledger you can only add to, never edit. Oruvenn
believes the same thing about itself. The people keep this belief in ordinary language:
a debt is "written," a vow is "cut" (as into stone), a death is "stood" (it now stands
in the record forever). The word for regret and the word for a closed account are the
same root.

Consequences for design (binding):

- No chrono-magic of any flavor. No rewind, no undo, no "it never happened," no
  prophecy loophole that rewrites events. Divination may glimpse a fated shape (as the
  Norse seeress glimpses a doom) but glimpsing it never lets anyone escape it.
- Hero battlefield revival is permitted but is strictly forward: a revived hero is
  alive-again-from-now, carrying every scar and memory of the death. Nothing is undone;
  a debt was paid and a new page begins. The campaign's one canonical permanent death
  is engineered to be un-revivable in-world (see the factions and campaign files) so
  that the Unturning has teeth the player feels.
- Items and abilities obey the Unturning. Emberhold wards preserve; Veilspun shades
  conceal; neither reverses. There is no restoration-to-a-prior-state effect anywhere.

## 3. What a no-take-backs people believes about regret

This is the thematic heart the doctrine asks for: what does a civilization believe
about regret when nothing can be taken back?

Oruvenn's answer is that regret is not a feeling you resolve; it is a weight you carry
and build on. Because you can never un-do, the culture has no concept of atonement-as-
erasure and no bargaining stage of grief. There is only what you do next. This forks the
two peoples along the single most consequential choice a no-take-backs mind can make:

- If nothing can be un-done, then make the NEXT thing unbreakable. This is the
  Emberhold answer. Regret becomes stone. You did not save the city; therefore you
  build a wall no fire can gutter, and you keep building, because the building is the
  only honest form of grief. The danger of this answer is that you wall your heart the
  same way you wall your town, and you never let anyone in again.

- If nothing can be un-done, then keep what remains hidden, so it can never be taken.
  This is the Veilspun answer. Regret becomes concealment. You were seen, and being seen
  is what got everyone killed; therefore you weave shadow over what is precious and you
  keep the names of the lost so they are never un-written from memory. The danger of this
  answer is that you hide truth from the living to protect the dead, and you mistake a
  secret for safety.

Both are grief. Both are correct responses to the same true law. The campaign's tragedy
is that two right answers, given by two halves of one bereaved family, cannot both hold
the same crossing -- and in a world without take-backs, the cost of learning to share it
can never be refunded.

## 4. The two magics (bounded by the Unturning)

- Wardsmithing (Emberhold). Forge-born magic that binds a protective ward into stone
  and steel: walls that do not fall, hearths that do not gutter, shields raised over
  others. It is preservative, slow, communal, and costly to raise and to hold. A ward,
  once sworn over living people, is bound by faith: to lower it while any it shelters
  still stands is to break the vow that made it. This oath-logic is the mechanism of the
  campaign's permanent loss (Sister Miren cannot drop the ward she has sworn over the
  refugee column, and is killed holding it).

- Shadeweaving (Veilspun). Magic of the unseen: concealment, shadow, misdirection,
  and the keeping of memory. It hides, moves, and remembers; it does not raise walls and
  it cannot restore. The Court's Whisperers weave the names of the dead into a living
  memory-cloth so no one is ever un-remembered -- their answer to a world where the dead
  cannot come back is to make sure the dead are never lost twice.

Neither magic can reverse time, undo an act, or restore a prior state. Both are central
and permitted (#1848 rule 2 and 3).
