# M1 Factions -- Emberhold Compact and Veilspun Court

Section of the Milestone-1 story bible (docs/story/m1-story-bible.md). Doctrine: #1848.

## 0. The shared wound: the Guttering and the fall of Corramere

Both peoples descend from one hearth-city, Corramere, built around a single great
forge-hearth that never went out -- the literal and civic heart of the people. In one
night, the Guttering, the hearth was put out and the city burned. (What caused the
Guttering is left partly unwritten for later milestones; in M1 canon it was a
catastrophe of the city's own overreaching magic, not an outside monster -- there is no
evil race here.) The survivors carried the same unbearable fact out of the ash: we were
whole, and now we are not, and by the Unturning it can never be un-happened.

They did not split over who to blame. They split over what grief demands. That is why
their war is a tragedy and not a morality play: they are the same family answering the
same question two honest ways.

## 1. Emberhold Compact -- the people who build

Identity: disciplined fortress-builders. Tanky, slow, methodical. They compact
themselves together behind walls and swear a Compact: never unwalled again.

What loss taught them. On the night of the Guttering, Corramere had no wall worth the
name -- it had never needed one; it trusted its hearth and its openness. That trust is
what the Emberhold read as the fatal error. Their doctrine is that safety is a thing you
BUILD, deliberately, in stone, before you need it, and that the honest response to a
loss you cannot undo is to make the next thing unbreakable. Every Emberhold structure is
a sentence in a single argument: this will not gutter.

Culture and voice. Plain, deliberate, oath-bound. Their sayings: "What is built,
stands." "Cut the vow, then keep it." They distrust speed and cleverness; they trust
weight and time. Their wardsmiths bind protective wards into keep and bulwark; their
Field Chaplains (like Sister Miren) raise wards over the living. Discipline is love
expressed as reliability.

Roster read (locked units, in character). The Shieldsworn, Halberdier, Ember Sentinel,
and Bastion Golem are a wall that walks; the Ashen Delver mines the stone the wall is
made of; the Ember Marksman and Siege Ballista are patient, torsion-driven range; the
Field Chaplain keeps the line alive. Structures -- Keep, Granary, Barracks, Forge,
Bulwark, Citadel -- are the argument in stone. Tech: Stonecraft, Field Drill, Forge
Mastery, War Doctrine, Ashen Ascendancy. Everything says: hold.

The flaw of the whole people. A wall keeps everything out, including help, including
grief, including the future. The Emberhold can wall their hearts as surely as their
towns, and confuse a fortress for a life. Their arc as a people (carried by Doran) is
learning that a wall you never open is a tomb you are still standing in.

## 2. Veilspun Court -- the people who hide

Identity: swift shadow-weavers. Fast, fragile, elusive. They spun a Court in the
shadow-hills and swore: never found again.

What loss taught them. The other half of the survivors read a different error in the
Guttering: Corramere was seen. It stood bright and open and known, and that is how the
catastrophe found everything precious in one place at one time. Their doctrine is that
what is hidden cannot be taken; you protect what you love by making it unfindable, by
scattering, by moving, by weaving shadow over the vulnerable. They are not cowards --
they are the parents who hid the children when the sky came down, and never stopped.

What they protect. This is the Court's secret and the key to their pathos: the Veilspun
guard a hidden remnant -- the youngest survivors of Corramere and their descendants, the
Court's future -- in shrouded refuges the Emberhold have never found. Everything the
Court does, every raid and every shadow, is ultimately about keeping that remnant
unfound. Their Whisperers keep the memory-cloth of the dead so the lost are never
un-remembered. They hide the living and remember the dead; that is the whole of their
faith.

Culture and voice. Quiet, allusive, watchful, quick to vanish. Their sayings: "What is
hidden is kept." "What is lost is lost -- so keep what is left." They value speed and
concealment because those are what saved the children once. Loyalty in the Court is
shown by keeping a secret, even a cruel one.

Roster read (locked units, in character). The Veil Gleaner, Duskblade, Nightstalker,
Umbral Skirmisher, and Thorncaster are fast, glass-fragile strikers who hit and vanish;
the Shadowmaw Beast and Gloomwing Raider are shock and mobility; the Veil Weaver holds
the shades together. Structures -- Sanctum, Waystone, Covenant, Loom, Bramble, Nexus --
are a web, not a wall; the Loom weaves the shades and the memory-cloth. Tech:
Shrouding, Swift Ways, Hexcraft, Night Pact, Eclipse Rite. Everything says: unfound.

The flaw of the whole people. A shadow hides the truth from friends as easily as from
enemies. The Court can hide so well that it hides from the living what the living need
to know, and lets people it could save suffer to protect a secret. Their arc as a people
(carried by Caul) is learning that some truths must be let into the light or the thing
you are protecting dies in the dark anyway.

## 3. Why the conflict is tragic-plausible, not good-vs-evil

The war is over Ashford Crossing -- the ford above the buried heart of Corramere, the
last place where the old hearth's warmth still seeps up through the stone. Both peoples
need it and neither can concede it:

- To the Emberhold, the Crossing is the one ground worth walling: the heart of the
  home they failed to save, which this time they will hold in stone. To give it up is to
  fail Corramere a second time -- and by the Unturning, there are no second saves, only
  second failures.
- To the Veilspun, the Crossing is the one place they must keep unfound, because their
  hidden refuges draw their warmth and their water from the same buried heart; an
  Emberhold fortress on the Crossing would, in time, expose the remnant. To let the wall
  rise is to lose the children a second time.

Each side is defending the survivors of the same dead city, by the exact method its
grief prescribes, on the exact ground that method requires. Neither is wrong from
inside. The catastrophe of the campaign comes not from villainy but from error under fog
-- a misrecognition in the dark (see Mission 3), which is the purest tragic engine and
the one thing the Unturning guarantees can never be taken back.
