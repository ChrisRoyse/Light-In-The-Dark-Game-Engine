## The one-paragraph pitch

Two peoples who were once one city are now at war over the ruin of that city. Long
ago the hearth-city of Corramere was put out in a single night -- the Guttering. The
survivors split by the shape of their grief: those who swore "never unwalled again"
became the Emberhold Compact, who build; those who swore "never found again" became
the Veilspun Court, who hide. Both are right from the inside. Their war meets at
Ashford Crossing, the buried heart of the dead city, which both need and neither can
give up. In this world nothing can be undone -- the Unturning is the first law of its
magic and its metaphysics -- so every death in this campaign is permanent, and the one
loss the player is made to watch and cannot prevent is the emotional spine of the whole
arc. The finale ends the war without ever un-writing that loss.

## How this bible satisfies doctrine #1848 (summary; details in each section)

1. Original and unrecognizable world. All proper nouns are invented and were run
   through franchise-collision web searches (see influence log). Names that collided
   were rejected and replaced (e.g. "emberwright" and the title "Ledger of Ash" were
   dropped after searches surfaced existing works). No orcs, no elves, no borrowed
   pantheon; the two peoples are one original people fractured by one original event.

2. Technology and magic envelope. Medieval base plus magic. Gunpowder is the hard
   ceiling: the Emberhold Siege Ballista and Veilspun raiders are torsion/mechanical,
   not firearms; there is no industry, no electricity. Magic (wardsmithing and
   shadeweaving) is central but bounded by rule 3.

3. Chronological absolutism. The Unturning is the world's first law: no time travel,
   no reversal, no undo, in lore, magic, hero abilities, or items. Hero revival exists
   as a battlefield mechanic but is alive-again-going-forward, never it-never-happened;
   the campaign's one permanent canonical death (Sister Miren, Mission 3) is explicitly
   un-revivable and stays lost through the finale. This is our emotional edge.

4. Sourcing rule. Only PD and CC0 sources were studied as structural lore inspiration
   (Aristotle's Poetics, the Kalevala/Kullervo cycle, the Norse Eddas, the Volsunga
   saga -- all public domain). No CC BY or CC BY-SA lore sources. The influence log is
   the legal-traceability record (not attribution).

5. Engineered emotion. Hero archetypes are built from published psychology and craft
   research on attachment, loss, and character change (parasocial-attachment studies,
   the identifiable-victim/singularity effect, prospect-theory loss aversion, the
   want/need + "the Lie" craft framework, and permadeath-grief-to-appreciation game
   studies). Every character carries a measurable attachment hook: competence plus
   vulnerability plus a loyalty display.
