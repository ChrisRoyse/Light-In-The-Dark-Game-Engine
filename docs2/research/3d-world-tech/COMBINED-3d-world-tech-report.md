# 3D World Technology: Findings & Recommendation

**Light in the Dark - Engine Technology Review - 2026-07-12**

Codebase + 363 open issues reviewed, plus web research across traditional rendering, the
triangle-splatting / Gaussian-splatting family, and diffusion world models -- all evaluated
against the binding constraint: **60 fps @ 1080p on a $500 Windows PC** (GTX 1650 / RX 6400 /
Iris Xe class), in Rust + wgpu + WGSL.

- `01-repo-and-issues-briefing.md` -- state & direction of the codebase and open issues

## Bottom line

Stay the course on the rasterized stylized-PBR renderer you already have -- it is the correct
architecture for the $500-PC target -- and spend the next quarter closing the **world layer**
gap (terrain runtime, GPU-driven culling, LOD pipeline, temporal upscaling), because that is
what the Thera world-mirror epics are blocked on. The exciting new tech splits cleanly:
**triangle splatting** is a promising *capture* tool worth one contained experiment;
**diffusion world models** are datacenter-only and wrong for a deterministic multiplayer RPG --
but **generative AI as a dev-time asset pipeline is ready now** and fits the "create high,
play low" model perfectly.

## 1. Where the engine actually is

The renderer is further along than "early engine" suggests. Already implemented and
FSV-verified in `litd-engine`: Forward+/clustered lighting with day-night and weather,
Cook-Torrance PBR with IBL texture slots, cascaded + point + spot shadow maps, an HDR post
chain (bloom, tonemap, color grade), instancing with frustum + HZB occlusion culling, GPU
particles/VFX, full skeletal + morph animation, a stylized WGSL shader preset system, SDF
text, and a real wgpu editor viewport. A quality-scaling system (`performance_min_spec.rs`)
and per-frame allocation budget exist.

The open-issue landscape (363 issues, 14 epics) says the direction is content and world: the
Thera world mirror (#1598, ~60 area children), stealth/perception (#1597), RPG systems, and
the WC3-parity editor. The rendering gaps that block those are specific:

- **No terrain runtime.** Epic #801's render children (#806-#817: tiles, cliffs, water,
  doodads, destructibles, weather...) are all open; procedural geometry (#1482) is open.
  Authoring tools exist; the shipped world layer does not.
- **No motion vectors / TAA / upscaling** (#1152) -- the highest-leverage min-spec feature is
  unbuilt.
- **The $500-PC target has never been tested on real hardware** (#1470). All min-spec
  evidence to date is simulated on the RTX 5090. By the project's own FSV doctrine, the DUAL
  budget (#928) is currently a hypothesis.

Constraints any new tech must respect: wgpu 27 + first-party WGSL only, permissive licenses
only (no GPL, no paid services per #1717), deterministic sim fed by frozen Calyx-lowered
data, stylized-PBR art ceiling (explicitly *not* photoreal), <500-line files, and FSV against
the real running app.

## 2. Traditional rasterization: what 2025-26 says ships on low-end

The industry's own low-end shipping record (Overwatch 2, RE Engine, Path of Exile 2)
converges on a clear recipe, and everything in it is implementable in wgpu today.

| Technique | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **GPU-driven culling** -- meshlet-granularity frustum culling in compute, two-phase Hi-Z occlusion, `multi_draw_indirect` | ADOPT | The "Capcom middle path": GPU-driven without full virtualization. Cheap on iGPUs; huge wins in dense cities/dungeons. wgpu supports MDI + count on DX12. Extends the HZB culling already present. |
| **Nanite-style virtual geometry** -- 64-bit-atomic visibility buffer, compute software rasterizer | SKIP | Requires 64-bit atomics absent on Intel pre-Arc iGPUs (Bevy's meshlets are Vulkan/Metal-only, not DX12); multi-ms fixed overhead eats the iGPU frame budget; multi-person-year effort still experimental in Bevy after 2+ years. Also solves a photoreal-density problem the stylized art ceiling doesn't have. |
| **Mesh shaders** | SKIP | Experimental in wgpu 28 (Vulkan-only for WGSL); absent on Iris Xe, GTX 1650. Not min-spec hardware. |
| **Baked probe GI** -- DDGI-style irradiance + visibility volumes, evaluated at half-res | ADOPT | Exactly what Overwatch 2 shipped in 2025 for hardware older than the min-spec (~0.5-1 ms). Bake day/night states offline. Bonus: the probe grid doubles as a queryable "light level at position" for stealth AI (#1597/#1601) essentially for free. |
| **Radiance Cascades** (Sannikov / PoE2) | WATCH | Constant-cost, no-RT-hardware GI with the right philosophy; but 3D world-space RC is still research-grade (Sannikov's sparse-3D demo is Nov 2025). Track as a v2 replacement for the bake. |
| **CDLOD / GPU-driven quadtree terrain** + chunked-clipmap streaming, geomorphing, vertical-error LOD | ADOPT | Modern consensus for huge worlds on weak GPUs; constant vertex budget regardless of world size. A Rust/wgpu-native blueprint exists (`bevy_terrain` / the Kuhnert UDLOD thesis). Directly answers epic #801 + #1482. |
| **FSR 3 upscaler via `wgpu-ffx`** | ADOPT | A Rust/wgpu-native port of the FSR 3.1 upscaler by a wgpu maintainer -- drop-in, MIT-licensed, pure shader (no tensor cores), satisfies the no-proprietary-blob doctrine. Render 720p -> present 1080p is roughly 1.8x GPU headroom, and it doubles as the TAA solution. Requires designing in jitter + motion vectors now. |
| **Virtual texturing** | WATCH | Only if terrain texture data actually exceeds VRAM; stylized art may get away with per-tile splat maps. |

**One flagged hardware risk:** Intel published a support advisory (July 2026) that valid
WebGPU indirect-draw workloads can hang UHD 7xx / Iris Xe iGPUs (device removed, no fix yet).
Since GPU-driven rendering is the core strategy, keep a CPU-culled non-indirect fallback path
behind a flag and test on real Intel iGPU hardware continuously -- which also resolves #1470.

## 3. "The new triangles and math": Triangle Splatting

The technology in question is **Triangle Splatting** (Held et al. 2025) and its 2026
successors **Triangle Splatting+** and **MeshSplatting**: instead of optimizing millions of
fuzzy 3D Gaussians from photos, they optimize millions of *plain opaque triangles*. The
output is an ordinary mesh that renders through the standard vertex/fragment pipeline with
z-buffering -- no per-frame sorting, no compute rasterizer, no CUDA at runtime. Performance is
startling: 2,400+ fps on a 4090, 220 fps at HD on a MacBook M4, and even the more expensive
stochastic variant runs on an Iris Xe iGPU. Triangle Splatting+ output even works directly
with a standard mesh collider (their Unity demo has walkable physics).

By contrast, classic **3D Gaussian Splatting** needs a per-frame GPU radix sort + alpha blend
and 200-650 MB VRAM per scene -- marginal at best on the min-spec. (If ever needed, Brush
(github.com/ArthurBrussee/brush) is an Apache-2.0 Rust/wgpu splat renderer to vendor rather
than rebuild.)

**The catch, and it's decisive:** the entire radiance-field family is a *capture* technology
for static scenes photographed in the real world. It cannot represent an authored fantasy
world, dynamic objects, destructibility, or day/night lighting (lighting is baked into the
representation; Triangle Splatting's per-triangle colors have no material model at all). No
shipped game uses splats as primary gameplay geometry as of mid-2026; the shipped uses are
backdrops, "skybox 2.0," and cinematic set pieces. There is also a licensing landmine: the
original Triangle Splatting trainer inherits Inria's non-commercial 3DGS license in part;
Triangle Splatting+ appears clean Apache-2.0 -- verify before commercial use.

**Verdict: EXPERIMENT.** Not a world representation for Ashen Reach -- the game is authored,
dynamic, and lit. But it is the first radiance-field tech whose output drops into the
existing mesh path at $500-PC speed. Worth one contained experiment (train a scene with TS+
on the 5090; the importer is days of work) for photoreal static set-dressing, backdrops, or
marketing captures -- and the field's direction (opaque, textured, budget-controlled
triangles) is worth tracking because it is converging on exactly what game engines want.

## 4. Diffusion technology

### Runtime diffusion world rendering -- NO

Every interactive world model in 2025-26 -- Google Genie 3 (~8 TPU v5 chips per session),
Decart Oasis 3 (cloud-only, ~$72/user-hour), Odyssey (H100 clusters, $1-2/user-hour),
Microsoft Muse/WHAMM (10 fps at 640x360), Matrix-Game 2.0 (the most efficient *open* model:
25 fps on a full H100) -- runs on datacenter hardware. A GTX 1650 is roughly 100-300x short on
compute and 20x short on memory, with no tensor path at all, against models whose coherence
horizon is still measured in minutes. Beyond compute, these models have no persistent game
state, no determinism (fatal for the multiplayer, replay/telemetry, and full-loot PvP
design), and no authored-content control. The labs themselves have pivoted their pitch to
agent training and AV simulation, not client runtime. Re-evaluate ~2028, and even then expect
"RTX 6090 feature," never "$500 PC feature."

### Neural rendering that does fit the min-spec -- two things

- **Neural Texture Compression, "inference on load" mode**: NVIDIA's RTXNTC decompresses to
  ordinary BCn textures at load time, needs only Shader Model 6, and is validated on GTX
  10-series / RX 6000 / Arc. 8-20x smaller texture shipping size. Worth prototyping once the
  asset pipeline (#1098) matures; check SDK redistribution terms.
- **Shader-based upscaling** (FSR 2/3 class -- already the Adopt above). DLSS, FSR 4, and
  neural radiance caching are all tensor-gated: skip at min-spec (DLSS opt-in only, per
  #1152's own framing).

### Generative AI as a dev-time asset pipeline -- ADOPT NOW

This is where diffusion actually pays off for this project, and it perfectly matches "create
high, play low": generation runs on the 5090 (or a hosted service), and what ships is
ordinary meshes and textures with zero runtime cost. Mature in 2026: **Meshy / Tripo**
(text/image -> PBR-textured props with clean commercial rights on paid tiers --
production-usable for stylized props and environment dressing with a Blender cleanup pass),
**Stable Diffusion / Flux** locally for textures and trim sheets, and **World Labs Marble**
($35/mo Pro: text/image -> downloadable 3D scene as GLB mesh or Gaussian splats) as an
environment-blockout tool. LLM-authored content data -- which the content-pipeline already
does for Thera areas -- is the quietly most practical technique of all. Two licensing traps to
avoid for shipped assets: self-hosted TRELLIS (texture stage depends on non-commercial
nvdiffrast) and Hunyuan3D (community license excludes EU/UK/KR sales). Note #1717's
zero-paid-services doctrine would need a carve-out or free-tier discipline for the hosted
tools.

## 5. Recommendation: how to proceed

Ordered by leverage; phases 1-2 directly unblock the Thera world epics and the min-spec
doctrine.

1. **Validate reality first (#1470).** Acquire one GTX 1650/1660-class or Iris Xe box
   (~$100-300 used) and stand up a real min-spec FSV rig capturing p95 frame-time at 1080p.
   Every claim below should be proven on it, per the project's own doctrine. This also
   surfaces the Intel indirect-draw driver risk immediately.
2. **Build the world layer with the proven low-end stack (epics #801, #1482, #1085).**
   CDLOD/quadtree terrain with chunked-clipmap streaming and geomorphing; meshopt LOD chains
   through the asset pipeline (#1098); extend HZB culling to two-phase occlusion +
   multi-draw-indirect with a CPU-culled fallback flag. This is 2-4 months of well-trodden
   work, not research.
3. **Design in jitter + motion vectors now, integrate `wgpu-ffx` (FSR 3) (#1152).** Cheapest
   ~1.8x GPU budget multiplier available; retrofitting motion vectors later is far more
   expensive than adding them while the terrain pass is being written.
4. **Ship baked probe-grid GI** (irradiance + visibility, day/night states baked, half-res
   evaluation) and expose the grid to stealth AI as the light-level query (#1597). Keep
   Radiance Cascades on the research shelf.
5. **Adopt the gen-AI asset pipeline at dev time**: Meshy/Tripo for stylized props into the
   glTF pipeline, SD/Flux for textures, Marble for blockout experiments. File issues under
   #1098/#984.
6. **Run one Triangle Splatting+ experiment** (a captured scene -> importer -> backdrop in
   the editor) to build hands-on knowledge of the tech most likely to matter in 2-3 years.
   Timebox it; verify the Apache-2.0 lineage.
7. **Skip, with written rationale in a doctrine issue:** Nanite-style virtual geometry, mesh
   shaders, runtime Gaussian splatting, runtime diffusion world models, DLSS/FSR4/NRC as
   defaults. Revisit diffusion runtime ~2028.

**Why this is the right call:** the 2025 shipping record is unambiguous -- the best-looking
games on weak hardware (Overwatch 2, PoE2, RE Engine titles) win with GPU-driven culling,
baked/probe GI, aggressive LOD, and temporal upscaling, not with virtualized geometry or
neural rendering. The stylized art direction (#928's explicit WC3-Reforged/LoL/Ori ceiling)
makes photoreal-density tech doubly unnecessary. The novel tech worth money today runs at
*dev time* on the 5090 -- exactly the asymmetry the "create high, play low" business model was
designed around.

# Briefing: State & Direction -- Light in the Dark Game Engine

**Research date: 2026-07-12. Source: codebase review + 363 open GitHub issues.**

## 0. What the repo is at a glance

A **proprietary, no-code, AI-native game engine written in Rust**, plus its two products: the
**Editor** (a Warcraft-III-World-Editor-class creation tool) and **Ashen Reach** (an original
multiplayer, full-loot, roleplay-enforced RPG built *with* the Editor as the proof the tool
works). The entire thing is built **on Calyx**, a vendored grounded-AI substrate that is the
backend/knowledge layer. The README is explicitly the *vision*; the authoritative state is
**363 open GitHub issues** (a "living backlog of 400+ atomic tasks").

## 1. Architecture & workspace layout

**Two Cargo workspaces.** Root workspace (`crates/*`, resolver 3, edition 2024,
`LicenseRef-Proprietary`) excludes `Calyx/`, which is its own workspace.

Root crates:

- `litd-engine` -- the reusable engine library (render/sim/assets/anim/physics/audio/VFX/AI).
  ~596 `.rs` files.
- `litd-editor` -- the product built on the engine (egui shell + WC3-style no-code modules);
  also an **MCP server**. ~624 `.rs` files.
- `litd-editor-mcp` -- MCP surface for the editor.
- `litd-game` -- the runtime host that plays a packaged `.litdgame` (main.rs, runtime_host,
  runtime_renderer, runtime_state, runtime_audio). Small (~9 top-level files) -- the game is
  authored data, not code.
- `litd-hash` -- content hashing.

**Calyx** (`Calyx/crates/calyx-*`, ~26 crates) is the AI/data substrate: `aster`
(storage/vault = the engine DB), `forge` (math/CUDA), `registry` (multimodal lenses),
`sextant` (search), `loom` (associations/TCT), `assay` (measured "signal bits" -> grounded
balance/analytics), `lodestar` (kernel / AGI-search), `oracle` (prediction/recurrence
forecasting), `ward` (out-of-distribution guard, fail-closed AI gate), `ledger` (provenance),
`anneal` (self-optimization), `mcp`/`web-api`, etc. Extensive design corpus in
`Calyx/docs/dbprdplans/` (31 numbered PRDs) and `Calyx/docs/implementation/` (staged build).

**Dependency direction (binding, doctrine #1071):** game -> editor -> engine -> Calyx ->
permissive crates. Never reversed. Editor and game are **strictly separate**; the Editor is
built FIRST; Ashen Reach is built entirely *within* the Editor (no engine-side game code).

**Rendering stack:** `wgpu = 27`, `winit 0.30`, `glam 0.29`, `egui 0.33`, `gltf 1.4`,
`rapier3d 0.34`, `kira` audio, `meshopt`, `basis-universal`/`ktx2` (texture compression),
`wasmtime`/`wasmparser` (the trigger/ECA "WASM escape" hatch). **Permissive-only license
policy (#1073): no GPL/copyleft.** Determinism preserved via fixed-point sim and a "lowering
valve" (#725) -- the hot sim tick and render frame consume Calyx-**lowered**, frozen,
fingerprinted data and never block on live Calyx calls.

**Telemetry / balance loop (Assay/Lodestar/Oracle):** Assay produces measured signal bits
from matches/replays; Lodestar is the kernel that answers over the corpus; Oracle forecasts
(balance what-if, crash/desync recurrence). This is the intended offline replay +
balance-tuning loop (issues #907/#1680).

**ECS/scene model:** custom archetype ECS per doctrine; scene/world content lives in Calyx
(Aster vault) as "constellations," lowered to frozen runtime formats.

## 2. Current 3D rendering capability (from source, not issues)

The renderer is **substantially built and real** (actual wgpu passes with FSV readback), not
a stub. The `crates/litd-engine/src/render/` tree is large and organized around a first-slice
`render/mod.rs` ("create/configure a wgpu surface, clear, present, with source-of-truth stats
for FSV readback") plus these implemented subsystems, each with a `*_gpu_wgpu.rs` (real GPU)
+ image-readback + tests:

- **PBR materials** (`material.rs`, `material_pbr.wgsl`): metallic-roughness,
  Cook-Torrance/GGX; texture slots BaseColor/MetallicRoughness/Normal/AO/Emissive/Irradiance/
  PrefilteredEnv/BrdfLut (i.e., IBL slots defined).
- **Lighting** (`lighting.rs`, `lighting_forward_plus.wgsl`): **Forward+/clustered** light
  assignment, ambient/lighting rig, **day-night cycle + weather lighting**
  (`lighting_daynight.rs`).
- **Shadows** (`shadow.rs`, `shadow.wgsl`): directional **cascaded shadow maps (CSM)**,
  **point cube shadows**, spot shadows -- with GPU render + tile readback.
- **Post** (`post.rs`, `post_gpu_chain.rs`, `post.wgsl`): HDR/tonemap/bloom/color-grade
  chain.
- **Batching/culling** (`batching.rs`, `batching_culling.wgsl`): instancing, frustum +
  **occlusion culling via an HZB/occlusion grid** (`occlusion_grid.rs`), deterministic
  material batching.
- **Stylized shader library** (`stylized.rs`, `stylized_effects.wgsl`): no-code preset
  library over a WGSL "shader system" with variant axes (`shader.rs`, `shader_system.wgsl`).
- **VFX/particles** (`render/vfx/`, `gpu_particles.wgsl`): GPU particles, cues, light atlas,
  workload budgeting.
- **Animation** (`animation/`, `skinning.wgsl`, `morphing.wgsl`): skeletal skinning (GPU),
  morph targets, blend trees, state machines, root motion, montages, gltf loader.
- **Sprites/billboards, screen-space effects (SSAO-class), primitives** (`primitive_mesh.rs`
  -- parametric mesh gen), **camera** incl. **cinematic camera**, **fonts** (SDF, rustybuzz
  shaping), and **UI/HUD/minimap/fog** (`ui_hud_minimap_fog.wgsl`).
- **Editor world viewport** (`editor_world.rs`): "real wgpu mesh/depth/fog pass for the
  center editor viewport" -- egui only composes chrome on top; it does not paint the world.
  Includes foliage instancing, layers, a reference scene, and visual presets.

**Quality scaling exists in code:** `performance_min_spec.rs` and `performance_quality.rs`
(+ tests) in the engine, and `runtime_renderer.rs`/`runtime_state.rs` in the game consume
tier settings. There is a **per-frame allocation budget** measured live on the 5090 (131,072
alloc/frame ceiling, ~4x observed peak) and a **perf overlay** (`ui_perf_overlay.rs`).

**What is NOT yet a full system:** a **terrain/world runtime** is only partly there. The
editor has terrain *authoring* (`terrain_advanced_tools.rs`, heightmap/pathing/foliage-brush
FSV examples, `asset_scene_composition_terrain.rs`), but the terrain **render** children of
epic #801 (tiles/cliffs/water/doodads/destructibles/pathing/regions/cameras/weather/
lighting-day-night/minimap/start-locations, #806-#817) are **all open**, and **procedural
geometry generation (#1482)** -- heightfield->mesh with per-tile LOD and splat -- is open.
Approximate style is **"forward" (Forward+/clustered)**, not deferred. Upscaling
(DLSS/FSR/TAA, #1152/#1091) is **not built** (explicitly "optional, later," needs motion
vectors + jitter first).

**Shader tech:** all WGSL, all first-party (16 `.wgsl` files). No external shader language or
DCC pipeline dependency.

## 3. Performance budget -- the DUAL target (issue #928)

**#928 is the binding budget doctrine, "create high, play low" (Battle.net model):**

| Machine | Target |
|---|---|
| **Creator / Editor** (dev box) | AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D (16C/32T, AVX-512) - **RTX 5090 32 GB** - 128 GB DDR5 - Win 11. "Max it out" -- push the 5090 to its real-time ceiling at 1440p/4K; full lens roster resident for Calyx. |
| **Player / produced GAMES** (min-spec) | **~60 fps @ 1080p** on a **$500-$1,000 PC**: Ryzen 5 / **RX 6600 or GTX 1660-class** / 16 GB, scaling up to ultra on a 5090. |

Visual-fidelity standard is explicitly **best-in-class STYLIZED real-time PBR** (reference:
Sunderfolk, WC3 Reforged, League of Legends, Wayfinder, Ori) -- **NOT** Unreal-5

end.

real min-spec hardware**. The environment has exactly one machine (the 5090 workstation);
#1153 (min-spec quality scaling) was closed COMPLETED but on 5090-only,
quality-scale-simulated evidence. Per the FSV-in-reality culture, that is "a hypothesis, not
evidence." #1470 requires acquiring a GTX1660/RX6600-class box and capturing p95 <= 16.6 ms @
1080p frame-time artifacts. **Any new rendering tech must be provable on that class of GPU,
not just the 5090.**

### (a) What this project is trying to become

A **single-machine, no-code, AI-native creation platform** -- a modern reincarnation of the

# State of the Art (2025-2026): Rasterized 3D World Rendering Feasible on a $500 Windows PC, in Rust + wgpu

**Research date: 2026-07-12. Web research (Exa + web search).**

Min-spec framing used throughout: Intel Iris Xe / UHD 7xx / Arc iGPU, AMD 780M/880M, GTX
1650 / RX 6400 class, all DX12-capable via wgpu, ~1080p target, no hardware ray tracing
assumed. Verdicts are for a small team building a large persistent RPG world (terrain,
cities, dungeons, stealth).

---

## 1. Virtualized Geometry (Nanite-like, meshlets, visibility buffer, mesh shaders)

**What it is.** Nanite-style rendering: preprocess meshes into ~128-triangle meshlets
organized in a simplification DAG, do GPU-driven per-cluster frustum/occlusion culling and
continuous LOD selection, then rasterize into a 64-bit visibility buffer (depth in high 32
bits, cluster+triangle ID in low bits, resolved with `atomicMax`), with a compute-shader
software rasterizer for sub-pixel triangles and hardware raster for large ones (GDC 2024
Nanite talk: https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2024/Slides/GDC+slide+presentations/Nanite+GPU+Driven+Materials.pdf).

**2025-2026 developments:**

- **Bevy virtual geometry (JMS55 et al.)** is the reference open implementation in the
  wgpu/WGSL world. Through 0.15-0.17 it gained METIS-based DAG building, per-meshlet
  compressed vertex data (unblocking streaming), a much faster software rasterizer, and
  BVH-based culling (0.17, by atlv24/SparkyPotato). It is *still experimental* as of Bevy
  0.18/0.19 (mid-2026): the 0.18 migration guide landed Dec 2025, and it was actually broken
  for two releases and only fixed again in March 2026 for 0.19 ("Fix Meshlets" PR #23307).
  Streaming is still not done. Sources:
  https://jms55.github.io/posts/2025-03-27-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-16/,
  https://jms55.github.io/posts/2025-09-03-bevy-fifth-birthday/,
  https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/pull/23307,
  https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/issues/11518.
- **Critically:** Bevy's `MeshletPlugin` requires `TEXTURE_INT64_ATOMIC` and **works only on
  Vulkan and Metal backends -- not DX12**
  (https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/pbr/experimental/meshlet/struct.MeshletPlugin.html).
  64-bit atomics on DX12 require SM 6.6-level hardware support; Intel's pre-Arc iGPUs don't
  expose 64-bit *typed* atomics at all, and even Arc/DG2 only exposes a subset via a vendor
  extension framework (https://github.com/GameTechDev/64-bit-Typed-Atomics-Extension). Docs
  confirm `SHADER_INT64_ATOMIC_*` and `TEXTURE_INT64_ATOMIC` need DX12 SM 6.6+
  (https://docs.rs/wgpu/latest/wgpu/struct.Features.html).
- **wgpu mesh shaders**: shipped as `EXPERIMENTAL_MESH_SHADER` in **wgpu v28.0.0 (Dec 18,
  2025)** -- full support on Vulkan, only *passthrough* (precompiled) shaders on DX12/Metal;
  WGSL support via `enable wgpu_mesh_shader;`
  (https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/releases/tag/v28.0.0,
  https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/issues/7197,
  https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/trunk/docs/api-specs/mesh_shading.md). Hardware mesh
  shader support also excludes Iris Xe/UHD iGPUs, GTX 1650 (Turing GTX lacks it on DX12), and
  RX 6400 has it but weakly. Mesh shaders are **not** a min-spec-viable foundation in 2026.
- **Nanite on weak hardware**: Epic itself acknowledges high fixed overhead on PC (multiple
  quarter-rate SV_Depth passes, no HiZ acceleration, empty indirect-draw chains) and ~50%
  occupancy on mid-tier GPUs
  (https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/nanite-sw-rasterize-high-register-use-dx12/2719076).
  Nanite's VisBuffer+BasePass costs several ms even on an RX 580-class GPU
  (https://crdg.jp/studio-tech-1/en/2025/05/29/3438/). Intel's own UE5 iGPU guide (June 2026)
  shows Nanite runs on modern iGPUs but needs software-VRS patches and careful budgeting
  (https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/technical/unreal-engine-optimization-chapter-2.html).
  Capcom's RE Engine (REAC 2025) instead ships a lighter meshlet pipeline using
  multi-draw-indirect + GPU occlusion culling without full Nanite-style SW raster
  (https://enginearchitecture.org/downloads/REAC_2025_Capcom.pdf).

**Effort:** JMS55's blog series makes clear this is a multi-person-year effort even with
meshoptimizer/METIS doing the heavy lifting, and it's still not production-ready in Bevy
after 2+ years.

**Verdict for min-spec: skip full virtualized geometry.** The 64-bit-atomic visibility
buffer path is blocked or fragile on exactly the worst-case hardware (Intel iGPUs on DX12),
mesh shaders are unavailable there, and the fixed per-frame overhead (1-3 ms+) eats an iGPU
frame budget. Take the *pieces* that are min-spec-friendly instead: meshlet-granularity
GPU-driven culling with ordinary indirect draws, aggressive precomputed LODs via
meshoptimizer, and Nanite-style two-phase occlusion culling. That's the
Capcom/Assassin's-Creed-style "GPU-driven, not virtualized" middle path, and it is well
within a small team's reach.

## 2. Global Illumination on Low-End

**Radiance Cascades (Sannikov / PoE2).** A probe hierarchy where near-field probes are dense
with few rays and far-field probes are sparse with many rays; cost is constant and
scene-independent, no RT hardware, no temporal accumulation needed. PoE2 shipped a
*screen-space* RC implementation (raymarching 4 rays at once into 128-bit occlusion bitmasks)
that runs on very modest GPUs -- 2D/flatland variants run at ~0.3 ms on a GTX 970
(https://github.com/Raikiri/RadianceCascadesPaper/blob/main/RadianceCascades.tex,
https://radiance.wiki/papers/sannikov-original,
https://80.lv/articles/radiance-cascades-new-approach-to-calculating-global-illumination).
2025-2026 developments: Sannikov demonstrated **sparse 3D world-space radiance cascades**
(Nov 2025, probes allocated only where visible) which attacks the main problem -- full 3D
probe grids' memory scaling (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGLAxW0xVDU). Caveats: full 3D
world-space RC is still research-grade; PoE2's shipped version leans on its fixed top-down
camera. The formal treatment (Osborne & Sannikov 2024) and community wiki (radiance.wiki)
exist; wgpu/WGSL implementations exist but are mostly 2D (e.g.
https://github.com/nixon-voxell/bevy_radiance_cascades, wgpu compute-shader ports).
**Verdict: extremely promising, iGPU-friendly cost profile, but 3D world-space RC is a
research project for a small team, not a checkbox.** Screen-space RC is more tractable but
suffers standard screen-space limitations for a free-camera RPG.

**Baked/probe GI (DDGI-style volumes, baked offline) -- the proven low-end answer.** The
single most relevant 2025 data point is **Overwatch 2's REAC 2025 talk**: they evaluated
RTXGI/DDGI, found runtime tracing infeasible on low-end, and shipped **baked DDGI** --
artist-placed probe volumes, irradiance+visibility (Chebyshev) textures baked offline,
evaluated at half resolution + temporal upsample on low-end. Cost: ~330 us full-res / ~115 us
half-res on a GTX 1080; after optimization (RG11B10 targets, fp16, split loops) the real
penalty on Switch-class hardware is ~1 ms, ~0.5 ms on Xbox One-class
(https://enginearchitecture.org/downloads/REAC_2025_Overwatch.pdf). A largely static world
(cities, dungeons) with day/night can bake multiple lighting states or re-bake probes
incrementally/asynchronously on GPU. DDGI's leak-resistant probe interpolation math is public
(JCGT paper, RTXGI SDK shader code:
https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/RTXGI-DDGI/blob/main/docs/Algorithms.md). **Effort:
weeks-to-a-couple-months for a competent graphics programmer; well-trodden.**

**Runtime DDGI (compute-traced, no RT cores):** Overwatch 2 explicitly found even the compute
fallback "not realistic" on low-end platforms. Skip.

**Screen-space GI:** cheap (sub-ms at reduced res, shipped on mobile/Switch-class hardware,
e.g. MF.SSGI runs on GLES3-class devices: https://github.com/MFrankfort84/mfssgi), good as a
*supplement* for dynamic near-field bounce and for stealth-relevant contact darkening, but
unstable off-screen and unsuitable as the sole GI.

**Verdict for min-spec:** **Baked DDGI-style probe volumes (irradiance + visibility) as the
backbone, ~1 ms budget at half-res; optional SSGI/SSAO layer on top; keep Radiance Cascades
as a v2 research track** (its screen-space form could later replace SSGI; sparse 3D RC could
eventually replace the bake). This combination is exactly what a 2025 AAA title shipped for
hardware older than the min-spec. Bonus for stealth gameplay: a probe grid gives a queryable
"light level at position" for AI visibility checks essentially for free.

## 3. Terrain & Large Worlds

**Modern consensus (2025-2026) for huge terrains on weak GPUs** is a GPU-driven
quadtree/CDLOD or clipmap with heightmap textures, geomorphing, and streaming -- not
tessellation shaders (unavailable in wgpu anyway) and not virtualized geometry.

- **Geometry clipmaps** (Losasso/Hoppe; GPU Gems 2 ch. 2): constant vertex budget (~500K
  verts for N=256, 8 rings) regardless of world size, toroidal texture updates, geomorph at
  ring boundaries. Still the simplest robust choice; a 2026 build log reconfirms it as the
  pragmatic browser/low-end choice
  (https://app.cinevva.com/blog/2026-02-25-open-world-browser-part-06-clipmaps,
  https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-i-geometric-complexity/chapter-2-terrain-rendering-using-gpu-based-geometry).
- **CDLOD / GPU-driven quadtree (best fit):** subdivide an implicit quadtree per frame in a
  compute shader, cull tiles, emit **one indirect draw** for all tiles with uniform vertex
  grids, morph LOD boundaries. A 2025 thesis (Kurt Kuhnert lineage -- the same author behind
  the Rust/wgpu **bevy_terrain** crate) formalizes this as "UDLOD" plus a **chunked clipmap**
  (node atlas + clipped quadtree lookup) for data streaming with graceful fallback to coarser
  tiles when streaming lags, and solves f32 precision at planet scale via Taylor-series
  relative-to-viewer coordinates (https://d-nb.info/1383361673/34). This exists in Rust/wgpu
  today -- it can be cribbed or vendored.
- **Vertical error / adaptive density**: Witcher 3-style per-region max-error metric to cut
  triangles on flat ground (~2x raster savings) (summarized with benchmarks incl. iGPU in
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KoAERjoWl0g).
- **Virtual texturing** for terrain splatting/megatexture: page table texture + physical
  atlas + feedback pass (render at 1/16-1/32 res, read back page requests) + LRU residency,
  Far Cry 4 "adaptive virtual texturing" style. A thorough 2026 explainer:
  https://www.shlom.dev/articles/how-virtual-texturing-work/ (note its honest caveat: VT is a
  *system*, worth it only when texture data truly exceeds VRAM -- for stylized art you may get
  away with per-tile splat maps + a small VT only for terrain albedo). wgpu supports
  everything needed (compute, storage textures, copies); there is no sparse-texture hardware
  path in wgpu, so it's the classic software indirection -- which is what most engines ship
  anyway.
- **GPU-driven culling for props/cities:** two-phase occlusion culling (render last frame's
  visible set -> build Hi-Z -> re-test everything against Hi-Z -> draw newly disoccluded) is
  now standard and implementable directly in wgpu compute (Bevy landed exactly this for its
  standard mesh pipeline in 0.16, PR #17413: https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/pull/17413;
  walkthroughs:
  https://medium.com/@Lucmomber/two-pass-hierarchical-z-buffer-occlusion-culling-93171c5a9808,
  original: Aaltonen SIGGRAPH 2015
  https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/aaltonenhaar_siggraph2015_combined_final_footer_220dpi.pdf).
  This matters enormously for dense cities and dungeons and costs almost nothing on iGPUs
  (Hi-Z build is cheap; the win is skipped vertex+fragment work).

**Effort:** CDLOD terrain + streaming: 1-3 months. Two-phase occlusion + indirect draws: 2-6
weeks. Virtual texturing: 1-2 months if needed. All mature, well-documented techniques.

**Verdict:** **GPU-driven quadtree terrain (CDLOD/UDLOD) with chunked-clipmap streaming,
geomorphing, vertical-error LOD, and two-phase Hi-Z occlusion culling for everything else.**
This is precisely the sweet spot: modern, scalable to huge worlds, and light on iGPUs. Use
bevy_terrain/the Kuhnert thesis as the blueprint since it's already wgpu/WGSL-native.

## 4. Upscaling on Low-End

Temporal upscaling is arguably the *highest-leverage* min-spec feature: render at 720p,
present at 1080p, ~1.8x GPU headroom.

- **FSR 2 / FSR 3.1 upscaler (analytical, open source, MIT):** runs on any GPU with typed UAV
  loads + RGBA16 UNORM (all min-spec hardware qualifies), no ML hardware. FidelityFX SDK
  ships FSR 2.3.x and FSR 3.1.5 upscalers as portable HLSL
  (https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK,
  https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/f4c1da8e/docs/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler.md).
- **The headline: `wgpu-ffx`** -- a **Rust/wgpu-native port of the FSR 3 upscaler by Connor
  Fitzgerald (wgpu maintainer)**. It takes color/depth/motion vectors, handles Halton jitter,
  and dispatches entirely through wgpu -- a drop-in for a custom wgpu engine, no C++ SDK, no
  backend interop (https://github.com/cwfitzgerald/wgpu-ffx,
  https://docs.rs/wgpu-ffx/latest/wgpu_ffx/). This removes what used to be the biggest
  integration obstacle.
- **FSR 4 / "Redstone" (2025-2026):** ML-based, effectively RDNA4-class; falls back to FSR 3
  elsewhere (https://gpuopen.com/learn/ue-fsr/). Not a min-spec tool.
- **XeSS 2.x:** DP4a fallback path runs on non-Intel GPUs and is very good on Intel
  iGPUs/Arc; Rust bindings exist as of Oct 2025 (`xess-rs`, Traverse Research:
  https://github.com/Traverse-Research/xess-rs) but they bind the native DX12/Vulkan
  library -- awkward through wgpu (needs raw handle interop). Optional nicety for Intel
  hardware later.
- **Frame generation (FSR3 FG/XeSS-FG):** requires swapchain proxying and is
  latency-sensitive; poor fit for wgpu and for a stealth RPG at low base framerates. Skip.

**Effort:** With wgpu-ffx: days-to-weeks (the real work is producing correct motion vectors
and jittering the projection matrix -- which is also needed for TAA anyway).

**Verdict:** **Integrate wgpu-ffx (FSR 3 upscaler) early and design the renderer around
jittered rendering + motion vectors from day one.** It doubles the effective GPU budget on
iGPUs and also provides the anti-aliasing solution.

## 5. wgpu/WGSL Constraints on DX12 iGPUs (as of wgpu 28/29, mid-2026)

What can be counted on across the whole min-spec (DX12 backend):

- **Compute shaders, storage buffers/textures, indirect draw & dispatch** -- core WebGPU,
  everywhere.
- **`MULTI_DRAW_INDIRECT` and `MULTI_DRAW_INDIRECT_COUNT`** -- native features, supported on
  DX12 (ExecuteIndirect) and Vulkan (https://docs.rs/wgpu/latest/wgpu/struct.Features.html).
  Note 2025 fixes: `vertex_index`/`instance_index` builtins for indirect draws on DX12 landed
  via a validation-shader trick (https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/pull/7535), and
  `multi_draw_indirect_count` had builtin gaps into late 2025
  (https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/issues/7974) -- test on real hardware.
- **Bindless (`binding_array`)**: `TEXTURE_BINDING_ARRAY` + `PARTIALLY_BOUND_BINDING_ARRAY`
  now work on DX12 Resource Binding Tier 3 (most DX12 hardware including Xe/RDNA2) (wgpu
  changelog:
  https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/0f37714fb8ed117f85f53b4f61ac70d4ec481a46/CHANGELOG.md).
  Usable, but keep a non-bindless fallback for oldest UHD parts (Tier 2).
- **64-bit atomics (`SHADER_INT64_ATOMIC_*`, `TEXTURE_INT64_ATOMIC`)**: DX12 requires SM 6.6
  + `AtomicInt64OnTypedResourceSupported` -- **absent on Intel pre-Arc iGPUs, spotty
  elsewhere**; wgpu emulates image atomics via RG32Uint on some platforms but with
  restrictions (https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/pull/5383,
  https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/pull/5537). Treat as unavailable at min-spec -> no
  64-bit-atomic visibility buffer. (A 32-bit depth-only atomic prepass + separate ID pass is
  the known workaround if SW raster is ever wanted.)
- **Mesh shaders**: experimental, Vulkan-only for WGSL-authored shaders (see Section 1). Not
  min-spec.
- **Driver reality check:** Intel published a support article (July 2026) acknowledging that
  a valid WebGPU **indirect-draw workload hangs UHD 7xx / Iris Xe iGPUs**
  (DXGI_ERROR_DEVICE_REMOVED, command allocator failure) with no fix yet
  (https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/support/articles/000102979/graphics.html). Since
  GPU-driven rendering is the core strategy, (a) test on real Iris Xe/UHD hardware
  continuously, (b) keep a CPU-culled non-indirect fallback path behind a flag, and (c)
  consider wgpu's Vulkan backend as an alternative on Intel (Intel's Vulkan driver takes a
  different path). This is the single biggest practical risk in the whole plan.
- Also useful and broadly available on DX12: `INDIRECT_FIRST_INSTANCE`,
  `DEPTH_CLIP_CONTROL`, timestamp queries (for perf budget work), 16-bit float in shaders
  (`SHADER_F16`, big iGPU win per the Overwatch talk -- check per-adapter).

## Recommended Architecture (summary verdict)

| Area | Ship this | Skip / defer |
|---|---|---|
| Geometry | GPU-driven renderer: meshopt-generated LOD chains + meshlet/cluster frustum culling in compute + two-phase Hi-Z occlusion + `multi_draw_indirect(_count)`; CPU-culled fallback path for buggy iGPU drivers | Full Nanite-style SW-raster/64-bit visibility buffer; mesh shaders |
| GI | Baked DDGI-style probe volumes (irradiance+visibility, half-res eval + upsample, ~1 ms) + SSAO/light SSGI; probe grid doubles as stealth light-level query | Runtime-traced DDGI; 3D world-space Radiance Cascades (keep as research track -- watch Sannikov's sparse 3D RC) |
| Terrain | GPU-driven quadtree (CDLOD/UDLOD) + chunked-clipmap streaming + geomorph + vertical-error LOD; virtual texturing only if texture data demands it (bevy_terrain thesis as blueprint) | Hardware tessellation (not in wgpu), voxel/virtualized terrain |
| Upscaling | wgpu-ffx (FSR 3 upscaler) as the TAA+upscale solution, designed in from day one | FSR 4, frame generation, XeSS (optional later via xess-rs) |
| wgpu features | compute, indirect, MDI(+count), bindless-with-fallback, f16 where available | 64-bit atomics, mesh shaders |

This stack mirrors what actually shipped on low-end in 2025 (Overwatch 2's baked DDGI, RE
Engine's meshlet+MDI pipeline, PoE2's cascades philosophy of "constant cost, no RT hardware")
and everything in it is implementable by a small team in wgpu today -- with the one caveat
that Intel iGPU indirect-draw driver stability must be validated on hardware early and
continuously.

# Research Report: Triangle Splatting, 3DGS State of the Art, and What It Means for a Rust/wgpu Engine Targeting $500 PCs

**Research date: 2026-07-12. Web research (Exa + web search).**

## Executive summary

The "new triangles and math" technology is **Triangle Splatting** (Held et al., 2025,
accepted 3DV 2026) and its successors **Triangle Splatting+** and **MeshSplatting** (CVPR
2026). It is the branch of the radiance-field/splatting family most relevant to us, precisely
because its output is *ordinary opaque triangles* that any rasterizer -- including our wgpu
renderer -- draws natively, with no per-frame depth sorting, no custom compute rasterizer, and
no CUDA. For our min-spec ($500 PC, iGPU/GTX 1650 class), triangle-family methods are
dramatically friendlier than 3D Gaussian Splatting. But the entire family shares one hard
limitation: these are **capture/optimization technologies for static scenes**, not authoring
technologies. They are a way to get photoreal *static environments* cheaply, not a polygon
replacement for a dynamic, interactive game world.

---

## 1. Triangle Splatting and its 2025-2026 successors

### What it is

Triangle Splatting ("Triangle Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering," arXiv
2505.19175, 3DV 2026) is a differentiable renderer that optimizes a "triangle soup" --
millions of unstructured 3D triangles, each with vertices, color, opacity, and a smoothness
parameter -- directly from photos via gradient descent, the same way 3DGS optimizes Gaussians.
The key trick is a new window function built on the triangle's 2D signed distance field,
giving soft, differentiable edges during training that can be annealed to hard, opaque
triangles at the end.

- Project page: https://trianglesplatting.github.io/
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19175
- Code: https://github.com/trianglesplatting/triangle-splatting

### How it differs from 3DGS and why triangles matter

- **3DGS** = millions of semi-transparent anisotropic ellipsoids that must be **depth-sorted
  every frame** and alpha-blended in a custom tile-based compute rasterizer. Unbounded
  support, fuzzy "surfaces," no native depth buffer.
- **Triangle Splatting** = when trained with opacity -> 1, sigma -> 0 (their
  `train_game_engine.py` path), the output is a plain colored mesh (.off/.ply). It renders
  through the **standard vertex/fragment pipeline with z-buffering -- no sorting, no compute
  shaders, no special format**. This is the whole point: it's rasterizer-native and
  game-engine compatible by construction.
- Quality: beats 3DGS/2DGS/3DCS on Mip-NeRF360 LPIPS (~10% better than 3DGS) and matches
  Zip-NeRF perceptual quality indoors while rendering ~500x faster.

### Reported performance (the headline for our min-spec)

From the paper (Garden scene, ~2M triangles, off-the-shelf mesh renderer):

- RTX 4090: **2,400+ FPS at 1280x720**, 1,900 FPS Full HD, 1,050 FPS 4K
- MacBook M4 (8 TFLOPS): 500 FPS HD, 370 Full HD
- RTX 5000 laptop, Windows: 570 HD / 380 FHD / 290 4K
- Stochastic (semi-transparent) variant on **Intel Iris Xe iGPU (2 TFLOPS)**: 33 FPS HD / 23
  FPS FHD on a 4.3M-triangle scene -- i.e., even the *expensive* variant is
  borderline-playable on an iGPU, and the fully opaque variant is far cheaper.

A GTX 1650 (~3 TFLOPS) sits between Iris Xe and the M4; the opaque-triangle path should
comfortably clear 60 FPS at 1080p for scenes in the low millions of triangles. Sources:
https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vedaldi/assets/pubs/held26triangle.pdf, project page.

### Follow-ups (the ecosystem moved fast in late 2025-2026)

- **Triangle Splatting+** (arXiv 2509.25122,
  https://trianglesplatting2.github.io/trianglesplatting2/): fixes the two big flaws of v1 --
  adds **shared-vertex connectivity** (semi-connected mesh, not pure soup) and **enforces
  opaque triangles during training**, so there is no quality drop when moved into a game
  engine. State of the art in mesh-based novel view synthesis: 25.21 PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 with
  only 2M vertices (vs. MiLo's 24.09 at 7M). Trains in ~39 min (A100). Output files are
  **~30-50 MB per scene uncompressed** (Garden 43MB, Truck 30MB). They ship a **Unity demo
  with physics colliders and walkable scenes using the mesh directly** -- the mesh is solid
  enough for an off-the-shelf non-convex mesh collider. Code:
  https://github.com/trianglesplatting2/triangle-splatting2
- **MeshSplatting** (CVPR 2026, https://meshsplatting.github.io/, arXiv 2512.06818): same
  group; enforces full connectivity via restricted Delaunay triangulation -> **connected,
  colored, game-ready mesh**, 24.78 PSNR at 3M verts, ~100 MB, 48 min training, renders **220
  FPS at HD on a consumer MacBook M4**. Code: https://github.com/meshsplatting/mesh-splatting
- **DiffSoup** (CVPR 2026,
  https://en.papernotes.org/CVPR2026/3d_vision/diffsoup_direct_differentiable_rasterization_of_triangle_soup_for_extreme_radian/):
  "extreme simplification" -- **<20K textured triangles** with neural textures + binary
  opacity + a tiny deferred-shading MLP; beats 3DGS/TexturedGaussians at equal budgets, **146
  FPS on a MacBook laptop**, ~10x faster than CUDA 3DGS because there's no alpha sorting.
  Most interesting for *mobile/iGPU budget* scenes, but requires an MLP pass in the shader.
- Also in the family: **1000FPS+ opaque triangle optimization** (3DV 2026, doi
  10.1109/3dv69130.2026.00115) and **FLAT** (feedforward single-image -> triangle splats,
  arXiv 2606.24876).

### Tooling maturity and licensing

- Training code is PyTorch + a custom CUDA rasterizer -- you need an NVIDIA GPU **for the
  offline capture/training step only** (our RTX 5090 dev machine is fine). Runtime needs
  nothing special.
- **Licensing -- important nuance.** Both `triangle-splatting` and `triangle-splatting2` repos
  are **Apache-2.0** at the top level, BUT the original repo also carries `LICENSE_GS.md` --
  the **Inria/MPII non-commercial Gaussian-Splatting license** -- because the differentiable
  rasterizer descends from Inria's 3DGS code
  (https://github.com/trianglesplatting/triangle-splatting/blob/main/LICENSE_GS.md). Before
  shipping anything trained with their pipeline commercially, we'd need legal review of which
  components inherit the Inria terms (the trained *assets* from our own photos are generally
  considered ours; the concern is the training software). Triangle Splatting+'s repo shows
  only Apache-2.0. The 3DGS reference implementation itself is explicitly non-commercial
  without Inria consent -- a known industry pain point.
- Maturity: research-grade (repos ~1.1K/300 stars, 2 main contributors, active through late
  2025). No DCC-grade cleanup/editing tools yet like the splat ecosystem has (SuperSplat,
  Postshot). The v1 paper itself says game-engine visual quality wasn't the training target;
  TS+ fixes most of that.

## 2. 3D Gaussian Splatting state of the art, 2025-2026

### Compression -- solved problem

- **SOG (Self-Organizing Gaussians)**, adopted and open-sourced by PlayCanvas (Sept 2025):
  stores splat attributes as WebP images, **15-20x smaller than PLY** (a 1 GB church scene ->
  55 MB with no significant visible loss). Spec:
  https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/gaussian-splatting/formats/sog/ ;
  https://blog.playcanvas.com/playcanvas-open-sources-sog-format-for-gaussian-splatting/.
  Other formats: SPZ (Niantic), .ksplat, compressed PLY; `splat-transform` CLI converts among
  all of them (https://github.com/playcanvas/splat-transform).

### LOD / streaming for large scenes -- working

- **Hierarchical 3DGS** (Kerbl et al., SIGGRAPH 2024) established the LOD-hierarchy approach;
  **CityGaussian/CityGaussianV2** (ECCV'24/ICLR'25, https://github.com/Linketic/CityGaussian)
  do divide-and-conquer training + block-wise LOD for km2-scale city scenes; **A LoD of
  Gaussians** (2025, arXiv 2507.01110) streams 60M+ Gaussians out-of-core on a single
  consumer GPU.
- Production version: PlayCanvas **Streamed SOG** -- automatic LOD chains (halving Gaussian
  count per level), chunked spatial streaming, runtime Gaussian budgets (2-4M desktop, 1-2M
  mobile). https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/supersplat/streaming/. Also
  Nanite-style cluster-LOD UE plugins
  (https://github.com/TimChen1383/NanoGaussianSplatting).

### Dynamic scenes and relighting -- still research-grade

- 4DGS (volumetric video) is real but heavy: 4DGS-1K (arXiv 2503.16422) and Disentangled4DGS
  (343 FPS on RTX 3090) show speed is achievable, and UE plugins (MLSLabs) play back
  100K-Gaussian volumetric video at 100+ FPS -- but this is *captured playback*, not
  simulation. No "rig a splat" workflow exists in any production tool (aukimi production
  survey, https://aukimi.com/blog/gaussian-splatting-production-pipelines-2026).
- Relighting: lighting is baked into SH coefficients. Research (ROS-GS arXiv 2509.11275,
  LumiGauss, GaRe, Relightable 3D Gaussians) decomposes sun/sky/texture for outdoor
  relighting but nothing is production-ready. **Day/night cycles on a splat scene are
  effectively not supported today**; shipped projects fake it with lightness-probe grids for
  dynamic *objects* while the environment stays fixed.

### Engine integrations

- **Unity**: aras-p/UnityGaussianSplatting (canonical, MIT), gsplat-unity. 1M Gaussians is
  roughly 3-8 ms/frame on RTX 3070; 180-220 MB VRAM per 1M splats with full SH (60-80 MB at
  SH0). https://polyvia3d.com/guides/gaussian-splatting-unity-unreal
- **UE5**: no first-party support even in 5.7; Luma AI plugin (free) is the production path;
  community plugins (XVERSE XScene, MLSLabs -- min RTX 2060, Magnopus OKO ~7M splats @60fps).
  Splats bypass Lumen entirely -- no GI/reflection interaction with meshes.
  https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/gaussian-splatting-unreal-engine-5-capture-to-game-pipeline,
  https://www.magnopus.com/blog/how-i-ended-up-rendering-unlimited-gaussian-splats-in-unreal
- **Rust/wgpu -- directly relevant to us**: **Brush** by Arthur Brussee
  (https://github.com/ArthurBrussee/brush) is a full 3DGS **training + rendering engine in
  Rust/wgpu/WGSL** (Burn ML framework), **Apache-2.0**, 4.8K stars, 30 contributors, active
  (last push July 2026), runs on AMD/NVIDIA/Intel, Android, and in-browser via WebGPU. If we
  ever want splats in our engine, Brush's `brush-render` crate (WGSL compute kernels:
  projection, radix sort, tile rasterize) is the reference implementation in exactly our
  stack, license-clean.

### Can 3DGS work on iGPU-class hardware?

Marginally, with aggressive budgets. Mobile deployments cap at 200-500K splats for 30 FPS;
PlayCanvas's browser games run a 1-2M Gaussian budget on phones/Steam Deck; the per-frame GPU
radix sort + alpha blending is bandwidth-hungry -- the exact resource an iGPU lacks. VRAM is
the other killer: 200-650 MB for a room-scale scan vs 5-20 MB for an equivalent textured
mesh. On a $500 PC with shared memory, a hero splat environment would eat most of our frame
and memory budget. It's feasible for *one* modest showcase scene; it is not a general world
representation at our min-spec.

## 3. Other novel representations, 2025-2026

- **2DGS / surfels** (Huang et al., arXiv 2403.17888): flat oriented Gaussian disks; better
  geometry than 3DGS, standard route to mesh extraction via TSDF fusion -- but extracted
  meshes score badly for view synthesis (15.36 PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 vs TS+'s 25.21) and need
  post-processing + neural-field texturing. Superseded for game-export purposes by the
  triangle-splatting family.
- **Mesh-extraction pipelines**: GOF, RaDe-GS, SuGaR, **MiLo** (arXiv 2506.24096 --
  differentiable mesh-in-the-loop, best of the Gaussian->mesh methods) all produce
  7-33M-vertex meshes needing separate texturing passes; TS+/MeshSplatting beat them with
  2-3M verts and zero post-processing.
- **SVRaster -- Sparse Voxel Rasterization** (NVIDIA, CVPR 2025, https://svraster.github.io/):
  neural-free adaptive sparse voxels, no popping artifacts, Marching Cubes-compatible.
  Interesting, CUDA-based, research-grade.
- Also circulating: 3D Convex Splatting (CVPR 2025, same group's precursor), LinPrim (linear
  primitives), RadiantFoam, BBSplat (textured billboards), Textured Gaussians. None of these
  change our calculus.
- **"Polygon replacement" framing**: no credible 2025-2026 source claims these replace
  authored polygons. The consistent industry framing is "photogrammetry replacement" and
  "matte painting / skybox 2.0."

## 4. Critical assessment for GAME development

### What shipped products actually do

- **PlayCanvas FPS demo** (Apr 2026,
  https://blog.playcanvas.com/turning-a-gaussian-splat-into-a-videogame/, covered by Tom's
  Hardware): a real browser FPS inside a splat scan -- but note the recipe: the splat is
  **only the visuals**; collision comes from `splat-transform -K` voxelizing to a
  `.collision.glb`, navmesh from Recast over that proxy, and dynamic-object lighting from a
  pre-baked lightness-probe JSON grid. Every gameplay system runs on classic tech bolted
  underneath.
- Studios ship splats as: virtual-production backdrops, "skybox 2.0"/distant environments,
  cinematic set pieces, cultural-heritage walkthroughs (Yahoo/PC Gamer piece with scan artist
  Christoph Schindelar, June 2026 -- who notes splat game experiments run "really, really
  close" to acceptable on a Steam Deck). No major commercial game ships splats as primary
  gameplay geometry as of mid-2026.

### The four hard questions

1. **Authored content?** No. Every method here starts from *photographs of a real place*
   (or, with FLAT-type work, generative video priors). You cannot art-direct a fantasy
   dungeon in this pipeline except by building/photographing physical or rendered sets. For
   an authored game world, this is a capture tool, not an authoring tool.
2. **Dynamic objects / destructibility?** No. Splats and triangle soups are static;
   deformation/animation is research-only. Dynamic things are standard meshes composited on
   top.
3. **Day/night, dynamic lighting?** No. Lighting is baked into the representation. Triangle
   Splatting's per-triangle flat colors have *no material model at all* (no normals for
   lighting, no PBR), so a captured scene can't be relit either -- though because it IS a
   mesh, one could in principle bake lighting to vertex colors offline or run it through our
   lighting as unlit geometry.
4. **Collision?** Triangle Splatting+/MeshSplatting: **yes, directly** -- the opaque
   semi-connected mesh works with a standard mesh collider (their own Unity demo proves it).
   3DGS: only via derived voxel proxy meshes.

### Fit for our engine (Rust + wgpu, $500 PC min-spec)

| Tech | Hardware vs min-spec | wgpu/WGSL effort | Maturity | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Triangle Splatting / TS+ / MeshSplatting** | Excellent -- opaque triangles, z-buffer, no sorting; iGPU-viable (M4 220 FPS HD; even stochastic variant runs on Iris Xe) | **Days, not months**: importer for their .ply/.off + our existing mesh path; vertex-color unlit shader. Training stays offline on the 5090 (their CUDA/PyTorch code) | Research-grade tooling, but output is a standard mesh so downstream tooling is *our normal pipeline* | **Best fit in the family.** Viable now for static photoreal set pieces / backdrops. Check LICENSE_GS.md inheritance before commercial use of the v1 trainer; TS+ repo is clean Apache-2.0 |
| **3DGS (SOG, LOD, etc.)** | Marginal on iGPU: per-frame GPU radix sort + alpha blending + 200-650 MB VRAM/scene | High: WGSL compute rasterizer + sorter (weeks-months) -- though Brush (Apache-2.0, Rust/wgpu) is a ready reference/dependency | Most mature ecosystem (SuperSplat, Postshot, SOG, streaming LOD) | Skip as a core renderer at our min-spec. If ever needed, vendor Brush's renderer rather than writing our own |
| **2DGS / mesh-extraction (MiLo etc.)** | Output meshes are huge (7-33M verts) and need neural texturing | Moderate-high | Superseded | Skip -- TS+/MeshSplatting dominate this niche |
| **DiffSoup-style (<20K tris + neural texture)** | Very good FPS-wise; needs small MLP in fragment path | Moderate (MLP deferred shading in WGSL) | Brand new (CVPR 2026) | Watch -- attractive for low-budget distant LODs of captured content |
| **SVRaster / other exotics** | Unproven off CUDA | High | Research | Ignore for now |

### Recommendation

The honest answer to "should we adopt the new triangle technology": **not as a world
representation -- our game is an authored, dynamic, lit world, which this family fundamentally
cannot represent yet.** But Triangle Splatting+ / MeshSplatting is genuinely the first
radiance-field tech whose output drops into a plain wgpu mesh renderer at $500-PC
performance. The pragmatic play is a small, contained experiment: train one captured scene
with TS+ on the dev machine, write the trivial importer, and evaluate it as (a) photoreal
static set-dressing/backdrops and (b) a future capture pipeline for reference environments --
while tracking MeshSplatting's "real-time viewer coming soon" and the DiffSoup
textured-triangle direction, which is where the field is clearly heading (opaque, textured,
budget-controlled triangles = exactly what game engines want).

## 2. Neural rendering that could actually help a shipped game on low-end hardware

This is where real, shippable value exists -- but tensor-core gating is the recurring trap.

**Neural Texture Compression (NVIDIA RTXNTC + DirectX Cooperative Vectors)** -- the most
relevant to us.

- Compresses texture sets 8-20x beyond BC7; demos show VRAM for a texture set dropping from
  6.5GB to ~1GB
  (https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/nvidia-ai-tech-claims-to-slash-vram-usage-by-85-percent-with-zero-quality-loss-neural-texture-compression-demo-reveals-stunning-visual-parity-between-6-5gb-of-memory-and-970mb,
  https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/benchmarking-nvidias-rtx-neural-texture-compression-tech-that-can-reduce-vram-usage-by-over-80-percent).
- **Crucially, it has non-tensor fallbacks.** Per the RTXNTC SDK
  (https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXNTC): "inference on load" (decompress NTC -> BC at load
  time) needs only Shader Model 6 and is **validated on GTX 1000-series, Radeon RX 6000, and
  Intel Arc**; "inference on sample" (decode in the pixel shader) works via DP4a/integer-math
  fallback but is 2-4x slower than the Cooperative Vector path on Ada/Blackwell. Cooperative
  Vectors exist in both DX12 (Agility SDK preview) and **Vulkan** -- relevant since wgpu sits
  on Vulkan/DX12; native extension access (wgpu-hal) would be needed since wgpu doesn't
  expose coop-vectors yet.
- **Fit for us: "inference on load" mode is genuinely viable on a GTX 1650** -- ship NTC files
  (smaller downloads/disk), transcode to BCn at load. On-sample inference on min-spec: no.
- **Verdict: the one neural-rendering tech worth prototyping in 2026.** Medium integration
  effort; SDK is source-available under NVIDIA RTX SDK license (check redistribution terms).

**Learned upscaling.**

- **DLSS**: requires RTX tensor cores -- locked out on GTX 1650. Dead for min-spec.
- **FSR 4**: now requires RDNA4 AI accelerators. Dead for min-spec. **FSR 2/3.1** remain
  pure-shader, run anywhere, and are open source (MIT) -- the practical answer for a wgpu
  engine.
- **Intel XeSS (DP4a path)**: the only *AI* upscaler that runs on non-tensor hardware -- DP4a
  fallback covers GTX 10-series+, RX 6000+. Caveats: GTX 1650 (Turing TU117) does support
  DP4a; quality/gain are lower on the fallback path; XeSS ships as a closed DLL against
  DX12/Vulkan
  (https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/what-is-super-resolution-nvidia-dlss-amd-fsr-intel-xess-and-microsoft-directsr-explained).
- **Verdict: ship FSR 2/3-class shader upscaling (or a hand-rolled TAAU) as the baseline;
  consider XeSS-DP4a as an optional quality tier.** Mature, proven, and integration into wgpu
  is very doable.

**Neural Radiance Cache (RTXGI 2.0 NRC)**: trains a tiny MLP per-frame to cache path-traced
radiance. **Hard-requires RTX tensor cores + ray tracing** (https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXGI).
Its sibling **SHaRC (Spatial Hash Radiance Cache)** is vendor-neutral and non-neural -- but
both presuppose a path tracer, which the min-spec can't run anyway. **Verdict: irrelevant to
our game; classic baked/probe GI is still the right answer for stylized RPG on iGPU.**

**Neural materials/shaders (NVIDIA "RTX Neural Shaders", neural BRDF approximation)**:
research/SDK-preview stage, cooperative-vector dependent, no shipped titles yet. Watch, don't
build.

## 3. Generative AI as a dev-time asset pipeline (this is where the actual leverage is)

Runs on the dev machine or cloud, ships as ordinary meshes/textures -- zero runtime cost, zero
min-spec impact.

**Text/image-to-3D:**

- **Meshy** -- cloud, browser, text/image -> PBR-textured mesh, auto-rigging, 500+ animation
  presets, quad remesh. **Paid plans include full commercial rights.** Most
  game-pipeline-complete option; quality for stylized/low-poly RPG props is genuinely usable
  in 2026 (hero characters still need artist cleanup). (https://www.meshy.ai/compare)
- **Tripo 2.5/3.x** -- similar, fastest generation (~10s), good stylized output, commercial
  terms on paid tiers (https://www.tripo3d.ai/compare/tripo-vs-hunyuan).
- **Microsoft TRELLIS / TRELLIS 2** -- open weights, best free image-to-3D; needs a 24GB Linux
  GPU to self-host. **Licensing trap: core is MIT but the texture pipeline depends on
  nvdiffrast (NVIDIA Source Code License -- non-commercial without NVIDIA authorization)**
  (https://trellis2.app/blog/best-ai-3d-model-generator).
- **Tencent Hunyuan3D 2.1/3.0** -- strong open-weights shape+texture; self-host needs 10-29GB
  VRAM. **Licensing trap: Tencent community license excludes EU/UK/South Korea for the OSS
  weights** -- a real problem if selling there. Static meshes only, no rigging.
- Maturity overall: props, environment dressing, weapons, furniture = production-usable with
  retopo/cleanup pass. Topology is still auto-generated (fine for static, mediocre for
  deforming characters).

**Text-to-texture/material:** mature and safe -- Stable Diffusion/Flux + ComfyUI locally (runs
fine on a dev 4090/5090), Meshy's texture mode, Substance 3D Sampler's image-to-material.
Great fit for stylized RPG trim sheets, terrain albedos.

**Image-to-3D world scaffolding -- World Labs Marble** (launched commercially Nov 2025):
text/image/video/panorama -> persistent, downloadable 3D scene. **Yes, it exports Gaussian
splats** (SPZ/PLY, ~2M or 500k splats) plus collider meshes on the $20/mo Standard tier;
**textured GLB mesh + commercial rights need the $35/mo Pro tier**
(https://docs.worldlabs.ai/marble/export/gaussian-splat,
https://docs.worldlabs.ai/marble/export/specs,
https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/12/fei-fei-lis-world-labs-speeds-up-the-world-model-race-with-marble-its-first-commercial-product/).
Caveats: worlds are walkable only within a bounded region; splat rendering in a custom wgpu
engine means writing/porting a splat rasterizer (doable -- reference implementations exist,
and 500k-splat scenes can render on modest GPUs -- but it's a distinct render path with its
own perf/lighting/collision problems). The GLB mesh export is the lower-friction route: treat
Marble as a **concept-to-blockout tool**, generate a scene, export mesh, retopo/stylize in
Blender.

- **Odyssey/Genie do NOT give exportable assets** -- Marble is uniquely useful because output
  is persistent 3D, not a video stream.

**LLM-assisted procedural generation:** the quietly most practical technique -- use LLMs at
dev time (or offline batch) to author quest data, dialogue trees, item stats, area
descriptions, and to write/tune procedural generators. Zero runtime cost, fully deterministic
shipped output. The repo's existing content-pipeline approach (spec-authored areas ingested
as data) is exactly the shape that benefits.

**Verdict: this is the layer to invest in now.** Recommended stack for a stylized RPG on our
budget: Meshy or Tripo paid tier (clean commercial rights) for props -> Blender cleanup;
SD/Flux for textures; Marble Pro experimentally for environment blockouts; LLMs for content
data. Avoid self-hosted Hunyuan3D/TRELLIS for shipped assets until the licensing issues are
resolved.

## 4. Honest feasibility: realtime diffusion worlds on a $500 PC, 2026-2028?

**No. Not in that window, and it's not particularly close.**

The arithmetic: Matrix-Game 2.0 -- the *most efficient open* real-time world model -- needs a
full H100 (~1000 INT8 dense TOPS, 80GB HBM) for 25fps at modest resolution. A GTX 1650
delivers ~3 TFLOPS FP32, ~4GB VRAM, no tensor cores, no FP8/INT8 acceleration worth naming.
That's a ~100-300x compute gap plus a 20x memory gap, against models whose *quality* is still
below a 2010 game engine in coherence, resolution, and interaction fidelity. Distillation and
4-bit tricks are yielding maybe 5-10x per generation -- say the gap closes to "runs on a 5090"
by 2028 (plausible; Decart's own optimization stack and TechCrunch's coverage point that
way). Min-spec $500 hardware also barely improves: it has no NPU/tensor path at all. Decart's
CEO himself flags the fundamental architecture cost: ~8,000 tokens *per frame*,
autoregressive, context filling in seconds
(https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/10/decarts-new-world-model-can-simulate-hours-of-photorealistic-driving-with-some-caveats/).

Beyond compute, the unsolved product problems: no persistent game state, no determinism
(saves, multiplayer, replays -- our engine has a replay/telemetry system that would be
impossible), coherence horizons of minutes, no authored-content control, and legibility/UI
generation failures (DeepMind lists these as "ongoing research challenges").

What informed people actually say:

- **John Carmack & Tim Sweeney** (on the WHAMM Quake II demo, Apr 2025): both *defended the
  research* against job-loss outrage -- Carmack called it "impressive" and analogized to
  engines replacing hand-coded rendering -- but framed it strictly as a **tool trajectory**,
  with Carmack noting the best games will still be built by dedicated teams; neither claimed
  neural frame generation replaces engines on any near horizon
  (https://www.techspot.com/news/107483-john-carmack-tim-sweeney-defend-microsoft-whamm-ai.html).
- Working game devs' consensus (visible in the Muse backlash,
  https://www.ghacks.net/2025/02/20/microsofts-muse-ai-model-faces-backlash-from-game-development-community/,
  https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/xbox/microsoft-xbox-showcases-an-incredibly-impractical-ai-model-for-gameplay-ideation):
  compute cost per user-hour is wildly uneconomical vs. a traditional engine that renders a
  better result on a $150 GPU, and the tech's real near-term customers are **robotics/AV
  simulation** (note Oasis 3 and Genie 3 both pivoted their pitch exactly there).
- The labs' own positioning confirms it: DeepMind pitches Genie 3 at *agent training*; Decart
  pitches Oasis 3 at *autonomous vehicles*; World Labs ships *exportable assets* rather than
  runtime streaming. The industry has already voted on where this is useful in the 2020s:
  **dev-time and simulation, not client runtime.**

### Bottom line for our engine

1. **Runtime diffusion world rendering: verdict -- no.** Wrong hardware class by 100x+, wrong
   economics, wrong fit for a deterministic RPG. Re-evaluate ~2028, and even then expect
   "RTX 6090 feature," never "$500 PC feature."
2. **Neural rendering on min-spec: verdict -- two things only.** (a) NTC "inference on load"
   for texture disk/VRAM footprint (works on SM6-class GPUs incl. GTX 1650, validated by
   NVIDIA on GTX 10-series); (b) FSR2/3-class shader upscaling, optionally XeSS-DP4a. Skip
   DLSS-dependent, NRC, and neural-shader paths -- all tensor-gated.
3. **Asset pipeline: verdict -- adopt now.** Meshy/Tripo (paid, commercial rights) for props,
   SD/Flux for textures, Marble Pro for environment scaffolds (splat or GLB export), LLMs for
   content data. This is mature, cheap, license-clean if you pick the hosted tools, and
   touches nothing on the runtime.
